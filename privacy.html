Your route is yours.

Effective: 21 July 2026 · Developer: Lantern Lion Games · Contact: dear.little.roads@gmail.com

This policy covers applications for and participation in the measured Dear Little Roads Android closed beta, including the exact Play-delivered 0.13.5/versionCode 27 build.

Website hosting

This public information site is hosted by GitHub Pages on the basis of our legitimate interest in providing a secure, reliable beta-information page. GitHub states that it logs and stores a visitor's IP address for security whether or not the visitor is signed in. GitHub processes that information under its privacy statement and may transfer personal data outside the European Economic Area using the safeguards described there, including the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and Standard Contractual Clauses. We do not receive GitHub's security logs; GitHub retains its service records under its policy. We add no website analytics, advertising trackers, account system, or non-essential cookies.

What stays on your device

Campaign progress, best move counts, Mailbag progress, sound and vibration preferences stay on the device. Dear Little Roads has no player account and does not use cloud saves in this beta.

Optional beta diagnostics

Dear Little Roads sends no analytics until you explicitly choose Share diagnostics. You can decline and still use the whole game, or turn diagnostics off later in About.

When enabled, the app sends a random app-profile code, random session and route-run codes, app version and beta cohort, timestamps and local date, allowlisted puzzle events such as starts, completions, moves, stamps, hints, resets and exits, active-time totals, Mailbag progress, settings state, sharing/review actions, and coarse technical-error categories.

Dear Little Roads does not include your name, email, Google account, contacts, messages, precise or coarse location, Advertising ID, Android ID, route drawing, individual taps, clipboard, typed text, purchases, or health data in diagnostic event properties. It also does not deliberately include a device model or full user agent in those properties. Like any HTTPS service, PostHog may transiently process ordinary network metadata, including an IP address and WebView user-agent header, to receive the request.

Why and where diagnostics are processed

Diagnostics are used to understand onboarding, puzzle difficulty, voluntary return, Mailbag use, and coarse technical reliability during the beta. Processing is based on your optional consent.

Allowlisted events are sent over HTTPS to a dedicated PostHog Cloud EU project hosted in Frankfurt. PostHog acts as the analytics service provider. Dear Little Roads uses no PostHog SDK, autocapture, or session replay. Every event sets $process_person_profile: false and $geoip_disable: true. PostHog is configured not to store the client IP with events, although it may process the IP transiently before discarding it.

Retention and deletion

The controller commits to deleting raw beta events no later than 60 days after the relevant beta cohort begins, after aggregate product decisions are recorded. Turning diagnostics off stops new collection and clears events waiting on the device.

After opting in, About displays a random deletion code. To delete already uploaded events, turn diagnostics off and send that exact code to dear.little.roads@gmail.com. Keep diagnostics off and do not reuse the code after requesting deletion. We will request deletion from PostHog and confirm only after the matching raw events are absent; PostHog may process event deletion on a schedule.

Beta recruitment information

When applications open, the form will ask only for adult eligibility, Android/Google Play access, Dear Little Roads exposure before seeing the recruitment page, comfort playing in English, coarse puzzle-playing frequency and genre interests, what attracted you, whether you work in a games-related field, your current expectation about remaining opted in for 14 days, recruitment source, and the Google-account email needed for closed-test access. It will not ask for your name, exact age, gender, address, device identifier, income, or marketing permission.

Applying will be voluntary and will not create a contract or guarantee an invitation. No answer will be required by law or contract. The marked answers and Google-account email will be required only if you want us to assess an application and, if selected, grant Google Play access; without them we cannot do that. Clearly ineligible visitors will be routed to a non-submitting end before their email or remaining profile answers are requested.

We will use submitted answers only to check eligibility, balance the small research cohort, grant Play access, send one installation invitation, send one optional feedback request, and communicate an essential build or safety notice if needed. This processing is based on our legitimate interest in running and evaluating a limited closed test with compatible, genuinely interested fresh players. That interest is balanced by collecting few fields, limiting access and retention, keeping participation voluntary, sending no marketing or return prompts, and allowing you to object or withdraw at any time. Lisa Wegel will review invitation decisions manually; there will be no solely automated decision with legal or similarly significant effect.

When applications open, Tally BV, Belgium, will provide the application, first-open confirmation, and optional unlinked survey forms as our processor. Tally states that form responses are encrypted and stored in Europe. We will not enable file uploads, tracking pixels, external integrations, or form-response email notifications. Access will be restricted to the organizer.

For selected applicants, Google services will process the submitted Google-account email so that we can send the invitation through Gmail and grant closed-test access through Google Play. Google may process information outside the European Economic Area under the safeguards described in its privacy policy and data-transfer frameworks. Tally and Google also process ordinary network and account data under their own notices when you use their services.

A separate optional first-open confirmation may reuse the submitted Google-account email and record only a yes/no statement that the game was opened for the first time plus its submission time. It is used only to count fresh first opens without requiring diagnostics. Not providing it does not affect access.

The optional post-beta survey will not be linked to an application or in-game diagnostics and will not ask for an email, name, app-profile code, deletion code, or unique invitation token. It will ask about the understood goal, early confusion, Mailbag use and route preference, reasons for returning or not returning, satisfying or repetitive parts, disappointment if the game disappeared, a non-binding monetization preference, and anything expected but missing. Free-text answers could identify someone if they choose to include identifying detail. Tally will also record a submission time and process ordinary network/service metadata under its own notice, so we do not promise technical anonymity. Because we will deliberately omit a response identifier, we may be unable to locate one response for individual deletion after submission; all raw responses will be deleted by the stated cohort deadline.

Non-selected submitted applications are deleted no later than seven days after selection closes. For selected applicants, organizer-controlled recruitment records, first-open confirmations, exports, selection notes, Gmail invitation copies and Play tester-list entries are deleted within 30 days after measurement closes. Raw unlinked survey responses are deleted by the same deadline after only aggregate counts and de-identified repeated themes are retained. We delete Tally submissions and empty its Trash, and delete Gmail copies and empty Trash. Providers may retain limited backups, security logs, or account records under their own published retention rules after our copies are removed.

Your recruitment choices and right to object

You may withdraw an application or object at any time to recruitment processing based on legitimate interests by emailing dear.little.roads@gmail.com. We will stop unless we can demonstrate compelling legitimate grounds or need a limited record to establish, exercise, or defend legal claims. Withdrawing does not affect any optional in-game diagnostics choice, and declining or withdrawing never limits ordinary support.

Sharing, sale, and advertising

We do not sell personal data, use it for advertising, or share it with data brokers. We use Tally, PostHog, Gmail, and Google Play for the limited beta functions described above. Those providers may also process service, account, network, security, or backup records under their own notices. GitHub Pages processes website-hosting records as described above.

Security and choices

Diagnostics and form responses are encrypted in transit. Access to the beta analytics project and recruitment records is restricted to the organizer. No method of storage is perfectly secure, so the beta deliberately minimizes what it collects.

You may decline diagnostics, switch them off, request deletion, withdraw from recruitment, leave the Play test, or uninstall at any time.

Children

This measured beta recruits adults aged 18 or older and is not directed to children. A future public release will be reviewed separately before changing the target audience.

Your rights and contact

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, delete, restrict, object to processing, and data portability where applicable. You may withdraw optional diagnostics consent at any time; withdrawing consent does not affect earlier lawful processing. You may also withdraw your beta application or leave the test at any time. Contact dear.little.roads@gmail.com. You may complain to your local data-protection authority.

Changes

If this policy changes materially during a later cohort, the new policy will show a new effective date. The measured beta consent screen and data inventory remain frozen during a cohort.
